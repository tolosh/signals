## Why FP1 dominates (diagnosis after every run; nothing changed)

A sample of FP1 cases shows the declaration in a form the table holds no alias for, while whole-
word matching needs the exact alias:

- legal names without their suffix ("National Australia Bank" against the table's "NATIONAL
  AUSTRALIA BANK LIMITED");
- split or joined spellings ("After Pay", "Klar Na", "n a b", "Money 3", "ZIPPAY");
- parent or sister names ("Block Inc", "Clearpay" for Afterpay);
- short forms no public source uses ("S1", "BFP", "nml", "Cld").

A public, cited table can't list most of these. What would close the gap is a matching rule
(spacing-insensitive tokens, alias prefixes without legal suffixes), which would be a new,
separately pre-registered experiment, not a change to this one.

## Limits

- Set v2.2 uses 83 alias pairs, repeated up to 12 times (D-L4-3).
- The table's compiler shares a model family with the set's author; its exclusion from the
  repository was by instruction. The 10 removed lenders' names are known to this detector's
  author; T8 checks the code holds no lender name.
- Lender identity for scoring is resolved through the table, so a table gap can split one lender in
  two on both sides; misses carry that tag.
- The categoriser is the local engine at taxonomy 1.4, given no biller code.
