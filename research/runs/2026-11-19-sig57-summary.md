## Why FP1 dominates (diagnosis after every run; nothing changed)

A sample of FP1 cases shows the declaration in a form the table holds no alias for, while whole-
word matching needs the exact alias:

- legal names without their suffix ("National Australia Bank" against the table's "NATIONAL
  AUSTRALIA BANK LIMITED");
- split or joined spellings ("After Pay", "Klar Na", "n a b", "Money 3", "ZIPPAY");
- parent or sister names ("Block Inc", "Clearpay" for Afterpay);
- short forms no public source uses ("S1", "BFP", "nml", "Cld").

**Completed from review 14 (S6), which found the sample above incomplete:**

- lenders absent from the table: 105 of the 248 unresolved identities;
- narrative words left in unresolved identities: 22 of 248 (for example `DD HUMM`, `TO SUNSHINE
  LOANS`);
- descriptor words on declared names;
- FN2 (67): 43 lines have no biller name and a non-lender category, 15 have biller names that
  don't resolve, and 9 are lenders that span several families.

**What a better matching rule could reach.** A spacing-insensitive rule recovers about 25% of FP1,
or 42 to 47% with prefix matching, giving precision of **about 0.51 at most**: still well below
0.80. Invented sister brands and initialisms are almost never recovered. Any such rule would be a
new, separately pre-registered experiment, not a change to this one.

## Deviations

- **D1, three uncited canonical names (review 14, S1).** Amendment A4 counts each lender's
  canonical name as an alias; for 3 lenders the canonical name itself has no citation. Precision is
  0.3217 with them and 0.3170 without.
- **D2, HUMM and PEPPER MONEY removed (review 14, S7).** The reserved-name test removed every lender
  whose name equals or contains a reserved unseen-lender name of any set. HUMM and PEPPER MONEY are
  reserved in earlier sets but seen lenders in v2.2, so they were removed though v2.2 doesn't
  reserve them. This is conservative: restoring them gives 0.3325.

## Design limits (review 14, S5)

- **The negative control can't support a refutation.** Rotating declarations can only lower
  precision, so the control passes (below 0.50) automatically whenever the primary is below 0.50.
  It adds nothing to a REFUTED result. Review 14 added a positive control: the generator's own
  catalogue as the alias index gives precision 0.9636, falling to 0.255 with rotated declarations,
  so the test can detect a good table. Future pre-registrations add a positive control whenever a
  negative control can pass automatically.
- **Recall ceiling 0.69.** Even with the catalogue as the alias index, recall reaches only 0.69 on
  v2.2, so SUPPORTED (which needs recall too) wasn't reachable on this set: the recall half of the
  hypothesis was effectively untestable here.

## Limits

- Set v2.2 uses 83 alias pairs, repeated up to 12 times (D-L4-3).
- The table's compiler shares a model family with the set's author; its exclusion from the
  repository was by instruction. The 10 removed lenders' names are known to this detector's
  author; T8 checks the code holds no lender name.
- Lender identity for scoring is resolved through the table, so a table gap can split one lender in
  two on both sides; misses carry that tag.
- The categoriser is the local engine at taxonomy 1.4, given no biller code. The category cache
  can't be regenerated without the author's Enrich engine (review 14).
- Some cited pages were unreachable at review; Wagepay SPV1 and Wagetap's licensee name are
  unconfirmed (review 14, S2). Review 14 found no contamination from the generator's catalogue:
  0.32 with the table, 0.96 with the catalogue, 0.20 to 0.27 with no table.
